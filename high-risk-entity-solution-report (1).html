High Risk Entity detection

This solution transforms a manual, analyst-heavy screening process into a structured workflow that combines historical intelligence, rules-based triage, guided LLM reasoning, targeted web research, and analyst approval. The result is faster identification of potential high-risk counterparties with stronger traceability and a more defensible review process.

Project plan

The proposed platform is not just a detector. It is a governed review system that starts from known historical knowledge, screens monthly counterparty populations in stages, and hands analysts a curated, evidence-backed queue instead of raw names.

Structured knowledge base

Historical high-risk entities, category mappings, blacklist entries, whitelist exceptions, keywords, and analyst playbooks are organized into reusable knowledge assets.

  • Category definitions and keyword groups.
  • Known high-risk counterparties by source and rationale.
  • Known safe exceptions that prevent repeat false positives.

Operational monthly workflow

The live pipeline ingests counterparty lists, checks known matches, applies rules-based triage, then uses LLM-assisted search and evidence gathering only where needed.

  • Easy pass and easy fail decisions are resolved early.
  • Borderline cases receive deeper enrichment.
  • Analysts review a ranked evidence pack instead of raw hits.

Analyst-ready output

The final interface presents suggested high-risk entities, category proposals, citations, and draft response support so analysts can approve, reject, or amend decisions efficiently.

  • Citation-backed recommendations.
  • Category-wise rationale and confidence signals.
  • Final approved HRE list plus draft response support.

Learning, training, and development phase

Before the live pipeline goes into production, the engagement begins with a structured discovery and knowledge-building phase. This is where existing analyst expertise is converted into reusable system intelligence.

1

Historical scan review

Review previously identified high-risk entities, past investigations, and prior scan outputs to understand what was flagged, what was confirmed, and what was dismissed.

2

Source and website discovery

Identify the websites, registries, and public sources where prior high-risk hits were typically observed. These become part of the evidence-gathering playbook for the live flow.

3

Historical category mapping

Collect counterparties that were historically tagged to categories such as betting, gambling, virtual assets, foreign exchange, and related high-risk classes. This becomes the starting blacklist and category library.

4

Whitelist design

Curate known counterparties that should not be repeatedly escalated, including legitimate firms, known safe entities, recurring false-positive patterns, and approved exceptions.

5

Analyst process mapping

Work directly with analysts to document how they search, what evidence they trust, how they classify, and where manual effort is concentrated today. This becomes the design input for prompts, rules, queues, and review screens.

Outcome of this phase: a validated domain playbook containing blacklist, whitelist, category definitions, source inventory, keyword inventory, and analyst decision heuristics.

Live monthly flow

The production flow is designed to resolve obvious cases early, spend effort only where needed, and deliver fully supported analyst recommendations.

Input

Monthly counterparty universe

Receive the deduplicated counterparty list extracted from monthly trade activity, along with transaction descriptors, client context, and any usable supporting metadata.

Step 1

Blacklist matching

Check counterparties against the historical blacklist and known category mappings. Strong matches are immediately identified as likely HRE candidates with known lineage.

Step 2

Whitelist and easy-fail controls

Known safe entities and approved exceptions are removed early to reduce noise and prevent re-review of recurring false positives.

→ Early deterministic triage removes obvious hits and obvious non-hits
Step 3

Rules-based scoring

Apply category-aware rules using names, descriptors, keywords, transaction context, and analyst-defined patterns to identify easy matches and borderline cases.

Step 4

LLM-based keyword reasoning

Use an LLM to interpret ambiguous names, evaluate contextual keyword meaning, and separate genuine business indicators from human names or innocuous strings.

Step 5

Web search and evidence retrieval

Search trusted public sources and relevant websites to collect supporting evidence on business activity, services, licensing, descriptors, and observed risk indicators.

Step 6

Citation and evidence pack

Bundle matched rules, snippets, URLs, category indicators, and extracted evidence into a concise review pack for every escalated entity.

→ Only the smaller set of uncertain or meaningful candidates reaches deep review
Step 7

Analyst review and decision

Analysts see the proposed category, supporting citations, and recommended action. They approve, reject, or amend the decision, preserving human oversight and governance.

Step 8

Final HRE list and draft response support

The approved set of high-risk entities becomes the final monthly HRE output and is also used to prepare draft responses or case notes for analyst completion.

Feedback

Continuous improvement loop

Approved and rejected decisions are fed back into the blacklist, whitelist, keyword inventory, rule library, and prompt guidance to improve future runs.

Analyst-facing experience

The intent is not to remove analysts from the process. It is to elevate them from repetitive searching to high-value decision-making with better context.

What the analyst sees

  • Counterparty identity and category recommendation.
  • Why the entity was escalated: blacklist hit, rule hit, or LLM-assisted evidence.
  • Citations and source links supporting the recommendation.
  • Proposed final action: approve, reject, recategorize, or request deeper review.
  • Draft response support based on the approved HRE list.

Business benefits

  • Lower review effort on obvious cases.
  • Higher consistency across analysts and monthly runs.
  • Stronger evidence trail for audit and governance.
  • Less dependence on tribal knowledge and manual web searching.
  • Faster case turnaround without sacrificing analyst control.
